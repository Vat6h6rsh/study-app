# Q2B · Questionnaire

Topic ID: `questionnaire`

## Concept

Open-ended questions allow a free response. Closed-ended questions restrict responses to stated choices. The upcoming exam requires exactly four of each: eight questions at half a mark each.

## Why it matters

Open responses reveal experiences and reasons. Closed responses allow comparison. Leading, loaded or combined questions produce unclear evidence.

## Step-by-step method

1. Write a short neutral introduction stating purpose and inviting responses from experience.
2. Choose four distinct aspects of the topic.
3. Write four open prompts using what, how or describe. A yes/no stem is not open merely because space follows it.
4. Write four closed questions with options: yes/no, multiple choice or a balanced Likert scale.
5. Number 1–8, label the groups and check exactly 4+4. Avoid overlapping ranges and give a clear time frame.

## Template / skeleton

```text
Intro: This voluntary survey seeks feedback on [topic]. Please answer from your experience.
Open 1: What difficulties, if any, do you face with [one aspect]?
Open 2: Describe an experience of [one aspect].
Open 3: What change would improve [one aspect]?
Open 4: How does [one aspect] affect [specific activity]?
Closed 5: Did you use [service] last week? Yes / No.
Closed 6: Main device? Phone / Tablet / Laptop / Desktop / Other / Not used.
Closed 7: Clarity? Very clear / Clear / Neutral / Unclear / Very unclear / Not seen.
Closed 8: Uses last week? 0 / 1–2 / 3–4 / 5 or more.
```

Tip: Replace every placeholder with task-specific content.

## Worked exam-style prompt

Prepare a questionnaire on students’ use of the digital library. Include a short introduction and exactly 4 open-ended and 4 closed-ended questions.

## Fully worked model answer

This voluntary survey seeks feedback on students’ use of the digital library. Please answer from your experience; you may skip any item.

Open-ended questions
1. What difficulties, if any, do you face when finding a reading?
2. How do you decide which digital resources to use?
3. What change would improve your experience of the library portal?
4. Describe an occasion when a digital resource supported your learning.

Closed-ended questions
5. On how many days did you use the portal last week? 0 / 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–7.
6. Have you received a portal orientation? Yes / No / Unsure.
7. How easy is it to find a book? Very easy / Easy / Neither easy nor difficult / Difficult / Very difficult / Not used.
8. Which device do you use most often? Phone / Tablet / Laptop / Desktop / Other / Not used.

## Why the model works

- The answer addresses students’ use of the digital library directly.
- Details support the requested task and each paragraph has a clear purpose.
- The model follows the requested format with precise, complete sentences.

## Common mistakes that lose marks

- Leading: “Do you agree our excellent system helps?”
- Double-barrelled: “Is it cheap and reliable?” Separate cost and reliability.
- Ambiguous: “Do you use it often?” Define a period and frequency options.
- Loaded: “Why do you waste time online?” Do not assume wrongdoing.
- Nine questions, eight yes/no questions or no options for closed questions.

Trap: A memorised answer on a related topic may still miss this prompt.

Remember: Check the finished answer against the exact instruction.
