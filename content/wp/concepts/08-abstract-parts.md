# Q5A · Write out abstract components

Topic ID: `abstracts`

## Concept

An abstract compresses background, purpose/aim, methodology, results/findings and conclusion/implications. Q5A asks you to write out four named parts worth one mark each. Enter sentence text, not a letter or label.

## Why it matters

Function matters more than position. One sentence can combine moves, and an abstract may omit a move. “We show” commonly signals a result rather than an aim.

## Step-by-step method

1. Read the four requested names first.
2. Identify context/problem and what the study set out to do.
3. Locate procedure, sample, tools or dataset. Separate what was done from what was discovered.
4. Find observed outcomes and their final interpretation.
5. Copy the exact relevant sentence(s). A sentence combining purpose and method may belong in both boxes.

## Template / skeleton

```text
Background: [Known situation / unresolved problem]. Signals: “Previous work…”, “Little is known…”.
Purpose: [Aim/research question]. Signals: “This study aimed to…”, “The purpose was…”.
Methodology: [How evidence was collected/analysed]. Signals: “We interviewed…”, “Data were collected…”.
Results: [Observed finding]. Signals: “We found…”, “The results showed…”.
Conclusion/implications: [Meaning/application]. Signals: “These findings suggest…”, “This may support…”.
```

Tip: Replace every placeholder with task-specific content.

## Worked exam-style prompt

Missed appointments can disrupt small clinic schedules. This study aimed to determine whether patients understood a revised reminder message. Researchers asked 60 consenting patients to explain the message in their own words. Fifty-two patients correctly identified both the appointment time and the rescheduling number. The findings suggest that the revised wording is understandable to most participants, although a larger sample is needed.

Write out: Background, Purpose, Methodology, Results

## Fully worked model answer

Background: Missed appointments can disrupt small clinic schedules.

Purpose: This study aimed to determine whether patients understood a revised reminder message.

Methodology: Researchers asked 60 consenting patients to explain the message in their own words.

Results: Fifty-two patients correctly identified both the appointment time and the rescheduling number.

## Why the model works

- Background: establishes the context or problem rather than the study action.
- Purpose: states what the investigation aimed to establish.
- Methodology: specifies participants, material or the procedure used.
- Results: reports the observed outcome rather than an intention.

## Common mistakes that lose marks

- Entering “C” instead of the purpose sentence.
- Paraphrasing when asked to write out source text.
- Calling every later sentence methodology.
- Inventing a missing aim or method. Old matching papers contain overlapping roles.

Trap: A memorised answer on a related topic may still miss this prompt.

Remember: Check the finished answer against the exact instruction.
