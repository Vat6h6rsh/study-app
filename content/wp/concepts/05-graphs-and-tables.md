# Q3A · Interpreting graphics and tables

Topic ID: `graph`

## Concept

A graphic answer selects data to answer the question. Bars compare categories; lines show sequences; pie charts show parts of a total; tables give entries. Read the command before describing.

## Why it matters

A complete description can still miss the question. If asked for a difference, calculate it. Do not invent causes unless the question asks for possible reasons.

## Step-by-step method

1. Read the command, title, period, units, legend, labels and scale. Identify approximate readings.
2. Write an overview of the main pattern first.
3. Select important highs, lows, changes, exceptions or comparisons.
4. Use labelled values exactly; use approximately for bar-height estimates. Calculate differences carefully.
5. Check every figure and qualification. Separate possible explanations from established observations.

## Template / skeleton

```text
Overview: The [chart/table] compares [measure] across [categories/period]. Overall, [main feature].
Evidence: A recorded [value and unit], compared with [value] for B. The gap was [difference].
Change: The figure rose from 80 to 100, an increase of 20 or 25%.
Verbs/adverbs: rose steadily; increased sharply; fell slightly; declined gradually; fluctuated; remained stable.
Prepositions: rose BY 20; rose TO 100; fell FROM 80 TO 60; peaked AT 120.
Close: [Synthesis answering the exact question].
```

## Worked exam-style prompt

Describe the overall pattern and compare the first and final weeks. Do not suggest causes.

Weekly library visits: Week 1 120; Week 2 160; Week 3 150; Week 4 210. Unit: Visits.

## Fully worked model answer

Library visits increased overall during the four weeks, despite a small interruption in the upward movement. The total rose from 120 in Week 1 to 210 in Week 4, an increase of 90 visits, or 75%. Visits initially climbed by 40 to 160 in Week 2. They then fell slightly to 150 in Week 3 before reaching the highest recorded value of 210 in the final week. The last increase, of 60 visits, was larger than the first increase. Week 1 recorded the lowest total. Overall, the final figure was 1.75 times the starting value, while the only decline occurred between the second and third observations. The graph records visits rather than distinct visitors, so it does not show how many individual students used the library.

- The answer addresses Weekly library visits directly.

## Common mistakes that lose marks

- BY for an endpoint or TO for the amount of change.
- 40% to 50% is 10 percentage points, or 25% relative growth.
- Treating groups as repeated observations of the same people.
- Adding separate comparison categories or treating visits as unique visitors.
- The old traffic prompt asks for reasons. Most other chart prompts do not.

Trap: A memorised answer on a related topic may still miss this prompt.
