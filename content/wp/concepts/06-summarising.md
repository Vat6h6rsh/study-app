# Q3B · Summary with title and keywords

Topic ID: `summary`

## Concept

A summary retains the main idea and essential supporting points in fewer words. Aim for about half the passage length unless a precise limit is supplied. Add a relevant title and highlight or underline keywords in your answer.

## Why it matters

Examples, repetition and minor details usually consume space without changing the message. Opinions and new explanations distort the source.

## Step-by-step method

1. Read the whole passage and state its main idea in one sentence.
2. Identify essential supporting points and qualifications.
3. Cut examples, repetition and decorative detail. Keep numbers only when essential.
4. Draft a connected paragraph in your own words and add a short informative title.
5. Highlight a few central concepts in the answer. Check the length and compare with the original.

## Template / skeleton

```text
[Specific title]
[Main idea in new words]. [Essential supporting point]. [Qualification or stated implication].
Highlight central terms such as ==accessible resources==. Do not highlight the entire paragraph.
```

Tip: Replace every placeholder with task-specific content.

## Worked exam-style prompt

Summarise the passage to about half its length. Give a title and highlight keywords in your answer. Include no personal opinions or minor details.

Source passage: A campus computer room previously replaced faulty devices as soon as users reported a problem. A new maintenance team now inspects each device before deciding whether repair is practical. The team records the fault, the expected repair cost and the safety checks required. Devices with serious safety problems are removed immediately. Other units return to service after testing. The team also tracks repeated failures so that future purchases can take reliability into account. The aim is to reduce unnecessary disposal while maintaining a dependable service. Students can report faults through a form that asks for the room and device number. The maintenance process therefore combines immediate decisions about individual devices with information that supports longer-term planning.

## Fully worked model answer

Repair Before Replacement

A campus maintenance team ==inspects faulty devices==, recording repair costs and safety requirements before deciding their future. Unsafe equipment is withdrawn, while repaired units are tested before reuse. Tracking ==recurring failures== also supports more reliable purchasing. The system seeks to ==reduce unnecessary disposal== without weakening service, linking individual fault reports with longer-term equipment planning.

## Why the model works

- The answer addresses main purpose, procedure and important qualifications directly.
- Details support the requested task and each paragraph has a clear purpose.
- The model follows the requested format with precise, complete sentences.

## Common mistakes that lose marks

- No title or highlighted keywords; highlighting only the source.
- “I think” or a recommendation absent from the passage.
- Copying sentences or producing disconnected fragments.
- Using an old 15–20-word instruction for a new half-length task.

Trap: A memorised answer on a related topic may still miss this prompt.
