# Upcoming exam strategy

Topic ID: `strategy`

## Concept and why

The instructor’s upcoming computer-based format is authoritative. Type answers into the designated text boxes. There are 5 questions, 9 parts and 50 marks. Assume 2.5 hours (150 minutes), as in the past papers. Confirm any final announced duration. Historical layouts supply practice tasks, not the blueprint for these mocks.

| Part | Task | Marks | Minutes at 3 per mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1A | Argumentative essay, 250 words | 6 | 18 |
| Q1B | Paraphrase a paragraph | 4 | 12 |
| Q2A | Formal email | 6 | 18 |
| Q2B | Exactly 4 open + 4 closed questions | 4 | 12 |
| Q3A | Interpret the requested graphic/table information | 5 | 15 |
| Q3B | Summary: title, highlighted keywords, no opinion/details | 5 | 15 |
| Q4 | Investigation report with all seven sections | 10 | 30 |
| Q5A | Write out four named abstract parts | 4 | 12 |
| Q5B | Full corrected paragraph; highlight every correction | 6 | 18 |
| Total | All five questions | 50 | 150 |

## Step-by-step method

1. Scan instructions and locate every answer box. Note each word limit and requested function.
2. Use the proportional budgets above. Within an 18-minute part, allow about 2 minutes to plan, 13 to draft and 3 to check. Review is inside the budget.
3. If quick recognition settles you, attempt Q5A and Q2B first, then Q4 while alert. Complete Q1, Q2A, Q3 and Q5B next. Alternatively, follow question order to reduce navigation. Keep the same time budgets.
4. Stop polishing when a budget ends. Leave a complete relevant answer before returning to refine it.
5. Check the mandatory details: 250-word essay; exactly 4+4 questions; summary title and keywords; seven report sections; exact abstract text; full grammar rewrite with changes marked.

## Typed answer skeleton

Plan → draft in the correct box → count words → check the task → proofread → mark required keywords/corrections → confirm the answer is retained.

In this app, select text and choose Highlight selection, or type ==keywords==. The preview renders a highlight. In the real exam, use its own highlighting or underline control if available. Do not assume this practice syntax will render there. If the exam provides only plain text, ask the invigilator which convention is accepted, state it briefly and follow that instruction. Do not rely on Ctrl+U without checking the editor’s behaviour.

## Worked exam-style prompt

You have the complete 50-mark paper and 150 minutes. Plan a sequential attempt.

## Fully worked model plan

Finish Q1A by minute 18 and Q1B by 30. Finish Q2A by 48 and Q2B by 60. Finish Q3A by 75 and Q3B by 90. Finish Q4 by 120, Q5A by 132 and Q5B by 150. Include proofreading within each interval. If Q1A overruns, finish its conclusion and move on instead of sacrificing a later part.

This works because every mark receives three minutes, all nine parts have a checkpoint and the 10-mark report receives twice the time of a 5-mark task.

## Common mistakes that lose marks

- Treating an old seven- or eight-task paper as the upcoming format.
- Spending half the exam on a 6-mark essay.
- Typing only an abstract label or letter.
- Describing an entire graph without answering its particular question.
- Omitting highlights or listing grammar fixes instead of rewriting.

Tip: Q4 and Q5B total 16 marks despite not appearing as full writing tasks in these old papers.

Trap: Citations/referencing are not in this rubric. Article writing and flowcharts are not in the exam.

Remember: Historical 15–20-word summaries remain labelled old exercises. Upcoming Q3B normally asks for about half length, a title and highlighted keywords.

Note: Writing rubrics are advisory self-assessment tools. Models show one good answer, not a unique correct response.
